Tutorial

Ten short steps show the main work in genie. Each clip shows genie with real data: the human genome hg38 at the BRCA1 gene, with tracks from the UCSC Genome Browser and exome reads of NA12878 from the 1000 Genomes Project.

1Open a genome

When genie starts, it lists ten genomes. Click hg38. genie gets the genome from the UCSC Genome Browser and shows chromosome 1 with its genes and its bands.

To use your own sequence, click Open reference and select a FASTA or 2bit file.

2Go to a gene

Click the locus box at the top, or push Ctrl+L (Cmd+L on macOS). Type a gene name, for example BRCA1, and push Enter.

You can also type a locus, for example chr17:43,044,295-43,125,364, or a variant ID.

3Compare loci

Type two to four loci with a space between them, for example BRCA1 BRCA2. genie shows them side by side. Each panel moves and zooms on its own.

Click the locus above a panel to show only that locus.

4Add tracks

Click the tracks button (the layers icon). The list has public tracks of the UCSC Genome Browser for the open genome. Click ClinVar, then phyloP 100-way.

These files are large, but genie reads only the parts that the view needs. The list also opens track hubs.

5Move and zoom

Drag the tracks to move along the sequence. To zoom at the pointer, hold Ctrl (Cmd on macOS) and turn the mouse wheel.

Drag on the ruler to zoom to a range. The − and + buttons next to the locus box zoom out and in.

6Open a file from the web

Click the link button, or push Ctrl+U, and type or paste an address. Here, it is the exome of NA12878, a CRAM file of 8.6 GB on Amazon S3. genie reads only the reads in view.

To open files on your computer, click the folder button or drop the files on the window. genie opens BAM, CRAM, VCF, BCF, BED, bigWig, Hi-C and many other formats.

7Read the details

Put the pointer on a variant, a read or a feature to see its card. The card shows the data of the file, for example the clinical significance of a ClinVar variant.

Click to keep a card open. Click a value in a card to filter the track by it.

8Change a track

Right-click a track for its menu: rows, colors, sorting, grouping and more. Each change shows at once.

Track settings… in the menu shows all settings of the track in one dialog.

9Find a sequence

Push Ctrl+Shift+F (Cmd+Shift+F on macOS) and type bases. A lane under the ruler shows the matches on the two strands.

Push Enter to go to the next match. You can allow mismatches, or type a pair of primers to see their PCR products.

10Save your work

Click the save button, or push Ctrl+S, to save the genome, the tracks and the view as a session. Open the .genie file later to go back to it.

Click the camera button, or push Ctrl+E, to save the view as a PNG or an SVG image.
