# Verification

This document tells which parts of genie have a formal proof, and which parts have none.

## What a proof is

genie uses the tool [Verus](https://github.com/verus-lang/verus). Verus reads a specification and the Rust code. It proves that the code obeys the specification for all inputs, not only for examples. If Verus cannot prove this, the check fails.

A proof is only as good as its specification. Read the specification to know what a proof shows. The specifications are in the Rust files, in the blocks that start with `verus!`.

genie as a whole is not verified. Only the parts in this document have a proof.

## How to run the proofs

1. Get Verus release 0.2026.11.02.8bf68ee from the [releases of Verus](https://github.com/verus-lang/verus/releases).
2. Put the directory of the release in `~/tools/verus`, or put it in your `PATH`, or give its path in the environment variable `GENIE_VERUS`.
3. Run `powershell -File tools/proofs.ps1` (`pwsh tools/proofs.ps1` on macOS and Linux).

The automatic checks run the proofs on each push. Verus uses Rust 1.99.1, the release that the file `rust-toolchain.toml` gives. The normal build does not use Verus: to the compiler, the code in a `verus!` block is plain Rust without its specification.

## What is proved

| Part | File | What the proof shows |
|---|---|---|
| The regions of a contig | `crates/genie-core/src/region.rs` | The start of each region is never after its end, and no operation has an overflow. `new` refuses a start after the end, and `between` puts two positions in order. The length is the end minus the start. Two regions with positions overlap exactly when a position is in both. The hull has each position of the two regions. The intersection has exactly the positions that are in both regions, and there is no intersection when no position is in both. The intersection starts at the later start of the two regions and ends at the earlier end. `clamp_to` keeps exactly the positions before the length. `expand` gives the region of its specification, also at the limits of 64-bit numbers. |
| Complements and codons | `crates/genie-core/src/codon.rs` | The complement of A, C, G and T is T, G, C and A, in the same case, and the complement of the complement of each byte is the byte again. The amino acid of a codon is the entry of the code table at 16 times the value of its first base, plus 4 times the value of its second base, plus the value of its third base, with T, C, A and G as 0 to 3 in upper or lower case: the place is never outside the table. A codon with another byte gives X. |
| The coding bases and the parts of amino acids | `crates/genie-core/src/effect.rs` | The coding length of a transcript, and the number of its exon bases, are the sums of the bases of its parts, or nothing when a sum is more than 64-bit numbers hold. The coding parts of a transcript are the positions that each exon shares with the coding part: one part for each exon that shares positions with it. genie puts the parts in the order of their start. For the bases from `from` to `to` of the coding parts, one after the other, `slice` gives parts with exactly `to - from` bases in all, and each part is inside a coding part, also when the exons overlap, as a damaged file can give. The base at each place of these parts is the base at the same place plus `from` of the coding parts. For the amino acids `first` to `last` of the protein, `protein_blocks` gives the parts of exactly their coding bases, in genome order. On the forward strand, the protein starts at the first coding base. On the reverse strand, it starts at the last coding base. Amino acids that are not all in the protein give nothing. The place of a position in the coding bases is the first place of a coding base at the position. Its c. number is the place of that base in the order of the transcript, plus 1. A position outside the coding parts has no place and no number. |
| The c. names of positions | `crates/genie-core/src/effect.rs` | The c. name of an exon base gives its place among the exon bases, in the order of the transcript. c.1 is the place of the first coding base. c.N is N - 1 places after c.1, for N from 1 to the number of coding bases. c.-N is N places before c.1, and c.*N is N places after the last coding base. For a position in an intron, genie finds the exon bases nearest to it on each side. It takes the nearer of the two, or the base before the position in the order of the transcript when they are at the same distance, as HGVS asks. The name is c.X+N for N bases after the exon base X, and c.X-N for N bases before it, in the order of the transcript. A position before all the exon bases, or after all of them, has no name. |
| The bytes of the bases of a FASTA file | `crates/genie-formats/src/fasta.rs` | The byte of each base is the byte that the index gives: the offset of the record, all the bytes of each line before the line of the base, and the bases before the base on its line. A later base is at a later byte. For a region, genie reads from the byte of its first base to the byte of its last base: the bases of the region and the line ends between them, and no other bytes. An index that gives no bases on a line, lines with fewer bytes than bases, or a byte past the end of 64-bit numbers gives an error. No arithmetic has an overflow, and no division is by zero. |
| The bins of BAI, CSI and tabix indexes | `crates/genie-formats/src/binning.rs` | A binning scheme has 9 levels or fewer under its top bin, and its top bin has 2 to the power of 62 bases or fewer. A scheme for a longer contig gets 9 levels, and the records past its top bin are then too far for the index. The number of the first bin of each level is the number of the bins of the levels above it, with no overflow. The bin of a record is the bin of the deepest level that has its first and its last base, as htslib gives it. On each level, the bins of a query go from the bin of its first base to the bin of its last base, and the bin of each record that overlaps the query is among them. Thus the bins that genie reads for a region include the bin of each record that overlaps the region. The level of a bin number is the level whose bins have the number. The bases of a bin, and its first window of the linear index, follow from its level and its place on the level. A number past the bins of the scheme, as a damaged index can give, has no level and no bases. |
| The chunks that genie reads for a region | `crates/genie-formats/src/binning.rs` | The bins of the index of a contig have their numbers in order, and no number is there two times. Two bins with one number, as a damaged index can give, are an error, as in htslib. For a region, genie takes each chunk of each bin, on each level from the bin of the first base of the region to the bin of its last base, if the chunk ends after the smallest offset that the index gives for the region. For a BAI or TBI index, this offset is the value of the window of the first base in the linear index, or the value of the last window when the linear index has fewer windows, or 0 when it has none. For a CSI index, this offset is the offset of a bin of the walk in the next row, or 0. genie then joins each chunk that starts at or before the end of the chunk before it. Each chunk that genie takes is inside a joined chunk. The joined chunks are in order, and they do not overlap. |
| The smallest offset of a CSI index for a region | `crates/genie-formats/src/binning.rs` | genie finds this offset with a walk along the bins, as htslib does. The walk starts at the bin of the first base of the region on the lowest level. From a bin, it goes to the bin on its left, and from the first of the eight children of a bin, it goes up to that bin. On each level, the bins of the walk are the bin of the first base and the bins on its left with the same parent. Each bin of the walk starts at or before the first base of the region. Of the bins of the walk that the index has, genie takes the offset of the bin with the highest number: this is the bin that the walk finds first. When the index has no bin of the walk, the offset is 0. The walk comes to an end, and no operation has an overflow. |
| The bases of 2bit files | `crates/genie-formats/src/twobit.rs` | Each base of a region is the base of its 2-bit code: four codes in each byte, the first code in the high bits, and T, C, A and G for the codes 0 to 3. genie gives one base for each position of the region, and no place is outside the bytes that it reads. |
| The N and mask blocks of 2bit files | `crates/genie-formats/src/twobit.rs` | The bases of a region that genie changes to N or to lower case are exactly the bases of the region in a block of the file. This is true for blocks in any order, as a damaged file can give, and each part is inside the region. |
| The numbers of CRAM files | `crates/genie-formats/src/cram/bits.rs` | An ITF8 number has 1 to 5 bytes and an LTF8 number 1 to 9 bytes, by the 1 bits at the high end of the first byte. The readers give the value of the bytes by the CRAM format: the value bits of the first byte, then the next bytes with the high bits first, and of the fifth byte of an ITF8 number only its low 4 bits. They give the number of bytes that they read. When the bytes stop before the end of a number, they give nothing, with no panic and no overflow. |
| The bit stream of CRAM files | `crates/genie-formats/src/cram/bits.rs` | The bit at each place is the bit of its byte, the high bit of each byte first. A number of `count` bits has the first bit highest, and it has 32 bits or fewer: for more bits, or when the stream stops before them, the reader gives nothing. A bit is 0 or 1, and a number of `count` bits is less than 2 to the power of `count`. The place after the bits is the place of the next bit. |
| The codings of the CRAM bit stream | `crates/genie-formats/src/cram/encoding.rs` | The canonical Huffman codes keep the bit lengths and the symbols of the header in their order. Each code has 31 bits or fewer, and no code is shorter than the code before it. More codes than 32-bit numbers can count are an error. The Huffman decoder gives the symbol of the first code whose bits come next in the stream, and it reads exactly the bits of that code. A Gamma code with z zero bits gives 2 to the power of z plus the number of the z bits after its 1 bit. A Subexp code with parameter k and no 1 bits gives the number of its next k bits. With n 1 bits, it gives 2 to the power of w plus the number of the next w bits, where w is k + n - 1. A Beta code gives the number of its bits. More than 31 zeros or ones, and numbers of more than 32 bits, are errors. For all inputs, the decoders have no panic and no overflow, and each of their loops comes to an end. |
| The rANS 4x8 decoder of CRAM | `crates/genie-formats/src/cram/rans.rs` | For all input bytes, the decoder of order 0 and of order 1 has no panic and no overflow, and each of its loops comes to an end. A frequency table has a symbol for each of its 4,096 places, frequencies of 4,096 or less, and the first place of the symbol of each place at or before the place. A table that gives a symbol two times, as a damaged file can give, is an error. A size past the limit of 2 to the power of 30 bytes is an error, thus no output has more bytes. The output has the size that the stream gives. |
| The rANS Nx16 decoder of CRAM 3.1 | `crates/genie-formats/src/cram/nx16.rs` | For all input bytes, the decoder of order 0 and of order 1, with the transforms PACK, RLE, CAT and STRIPE, has no panic and no overflow, and each of its loops comes to an end. A stripe can hold stripes to 4 levels. More levels, as a damaged file can give, are an error: thus the decoder calls itself 4 times or fewer, and the stack cannot overflow. Before genie makes an output of a size that the stream gives, it compares the size with the limit of 2 to the power of 30 bytes, thus no output has more bytes. A number of more than 32 bits is an error. A frequency table has a symbol for each of its places, frequencies of its number of places or less, and the first place of the symbol of each place at or before the place. The output of order 0, order 1, PACK and STRIPE has the size that the stream gives. |
| The virtual offsets of BGZF files | `crates/genie-formats/src/bgzf.rs` | A virtual offset is the offset of a block in the file times 65,536, plus a place in the uncompressed block. `pack` makes the virtual offset of a block before 2 to the power of 48 and a place before 65,536, and `unpack` gives the two parts back exactly. A `.gzi` index that gives a block at 2 to the power of 48 or after gives an error, not a virtual offset of a wrong block. |

## Holes

A hole is code or an assumption that has no proof. Each hole is in this list.

### Holes that genie can close

| Hole | State |
|---|---|
| The virtual offsets that the BGZF reader and writer give for their place, in files of 2 to the power of 48 bytes (256 TB) or more. | Open |
| The facts of the code table, for example its three stop codons TAA, TAG and TGA. Verus 0.2026.11.02 stops with an internal error when it computes with the bytes of a text constant. | Open, waits for Verus |
| The byte arrays of CRAM (ByteArrayLen and ByteArrayStop), the external blocks of a slice, and the records that genie builds from them. This code uses a hash map and boxes. | Open |
| The other code that has only whole numbers and bytes, for example the tandem repeats, the walk along a CIGAR, and the rows of features. | Open |

### Holes that stay

Verus cannot work on these parts now, or the parts are not genie's code:

- Floating point numbers, for example the statistics (Fisher's exact test, t-tests), the methylation fractions and the layout of the views. Verus has almost no support for floating point.
- The readers of text formats, for example BED, GFF3, GTF, VCF and the domain files. Verus has little support for the text functions of the standard library.
- The text that genie writes from numbers, for example the c. and p. names of a variant. The numbers in the c. names have a proof, but the text has none.
- Files, the network, the clock and other input and output.
- The window toolkit (iced), the drawing and the rest of the user interface.
- The libraries that genie uses, for example rustls and miniz_oxide.

When Verus supports more of Rust, a part from this list can get a proof. Then it moves to the holes that genie can close.

## What a proof depends on

A proof is correct only if these parts are correct:

- Verus and the Z3 solver that it uses.
- The Rust compiler.
- The specifications that Verus gets for functions of the Rust standard library and of `vstd`, for example `min`, `max`, `saturating_sub`, `saturating_add`, `checked_add`, `checked_mul` and `slice_subrange`.
- The lemmas of the Verus library `vstd` about division, multiplication, powers of two and bits.
- For the bases of a FASTA file: a file with the lines that its index gives.
- For the chunks of a region, and the coding parts and the exons of a transcript: the sort of the Rust standard library. It must keep each item and put the items in the order of their start.
- For the records of a region: an index that is correct for its file. genie reads each chunk that can hold a record of the region and that ends after the smallest offset of the index for the region. For a BAI or TBI index, the value of a window of the linear index must not be after the offset of a record with a base in the window or after it. For a CSI index, the offset of a bin must not be after the offset of a record with a base at or after the first base of the bin. If an index does not obey this, genie can miss a record of the region.
